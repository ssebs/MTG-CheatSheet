Card Types

the fundamental categories — what every card fundamentally is
▾
Sorcery Card type
A one-shot spell you can only cast on your own turn, during a main phase, with an empty stack. Goes to the graveyard after resolving.
"Sorcery speed" = your main phase, nothing else pending.
Instant Card type
A one-shot spell you can cast almost any time, including on other players' turns and in response to the stack.
The reason to always fear open blue mana.
Creature Card type
A permanent that can attack and block. Has power/toughness and is subject to summoning sickness.
Enchantment Card type
A permanent with an ongoing effect. Auras attach to something; others sit on their own.
Artifact Card type
A usually-colorless permanent — equipment, mana rocks, and gadgets. Can also be a creature.
Planeswalker Card type
An ally permanent with loyalty counters; activate one loyalty ability per turn. Can be attacked like a player.
Land Card type
A permanent that isn't cast — you play one per turn from hand, usually for mana. Not a spell, so it can't be countered.
Battle Card type
A newer shared permanent with defense counters that players attack to flip it for a reward.
Permanent Umbrella
Anything that stays on the battlefield: creatures, artifacts, enchantments, planeswalkers, lands, battles.
Legendary Supertype
You can only control one with a given name at a time; a second forces you to sacrifice one (the "legend rule").

The Turn, In Order

the five phases every turn runs through, start to finish
▾
1

Beginning Phase

The turn opens here, before you do anything active.

Untap step: untap your permanents (no one gets priority).  Upkeep step: "at the beginning of your upkeep" triggers go on the stack; players get priority.  Draw step: you draw for the turn.
2

First Main Phase Precombat

Play lands and cast anything at sorcery speed — creatures, sorceries, artifacts, enchantments, planeswalkers. Most people develop their board before attacking.

3

Combat Phase

Where attacks happen, resolved as an ordered sequence of steps.

Beginning of combat: last window before attackers.  Declare attackers: tap and choose attackers; attack triggers fire.  Declare blockers: defenders assign blocks.  Combat damage: damage is dealt (first strike gets its own step).  End of combat.
4

Second Main Phase Postcombat

Another full main phase after combat. Play a land if you haven't, deploy mana you held up, or rebuild after the dust settles.

5

Ending Phase

The turn winds down.

End step: "at the beginning of your end step" triggers fire.  Cleanup: discard down to your maximum hand size (usually seven) and remove "until end of turn" effects and damage.

Evasion & Combat

how creatures get through — and what happens when they connect
▾
Flying Evergreen
Can only be blocked by creatures with flying or reach.
A ground creature simply can't stop it.
Reach Evergreen
Can block creatures with flying, but has no evasion itself.
The classic spider answer to fliers.
Menace Evergreen
Can only be blocked by two or more creatures.
Trample Evergreen
Excess combat damage beyond a blocker's toughness spills over to the player or planeswalker.
First Strike Evergreen
Deals combat damage before creatures without it. If it kills the blocker first, it may take none back.
Double Strike Evergreen
Deals both first-strike and regular combat damage — effectively hits twice.
Deathtouch Evergreen
Any amount of damage it deals to a creature is lethal.
Pairs viciously with trample: assign 1, trample the rest.
Vigilance Evergreen
Doesn't tap to attack, so it stays up to block.
Haste Evergreen
Can attack and use tap abilities the turn it enters, ignoring summoning sickness.
Defender Evergreen
Can't attack. Usually a wall meant purely to block.
Flash Evergreen
Can be cast any time you could cast an instant, including on other players' turns.
Intimidate Older
Can only be blocked by artifact creatures and creatures that share a color with it.
Skulk Uncommon
Can't be blocked by creatures with greater power.
Horsemanship Rare
Can only be blocked by creatures with horsemanship — nearly unblockable in practice.

Protection & Resilience

keeping your permanents on the battlefield
▾
Ward Evergreen
When an opponent targets this with a spell or ability, that spell/ability is countered unless they pay the ward cost (mana, life, discard, etc.).
It doesn't stop targeting — it taxes it. Unpaid = countered.
Hexproof Evergreen
Can't be the target of spells or abilities opponents control. You can still target it yourself.
Shroud Older
Can't be targeted by anyone, including its own controller.
Protection (from ___) Evergreen
Can't be Damaged, Enchanted/equipped, Blocked, or Targeted by the named quality. Remember: DEBT.
Indestructible Evergreen
Can't be destroyed by damage or destroy effects. Still dies to sacrifice, −X/−X, or exile.
Regenerate Older
The next time it would be destroyed this turn, instead tap it, remove it from combat, and heal damage — it survives.
Totem Armor Uncommon
From certain auras: if the enchanted permanent would be destroyed, destroy the aura instead.
Hexproof from ___ Variant
Hexproof only against a specific color or quality (e.g. "hexproof from black").
Phasing Rare
A phased-out permanent is treated as though it doesn't exist — dodges removal, then phases back in on your untap.

The Fine Distinctions

the pairs people mix up — and why the difference matters
▾

Destroy

Sends a permanent to the graveyard. Stopped by indestructible; can be regenerated.

"Destroy target creature."
vs

Sacrifice

The controller puts their own permanent in the graveyard. Ignores indestructible, hexproof, and regeneration — nothing targets.

The cleanest way past protection.

Destroy / Sacrifice

Goes to the graveyard — where it can be reanimated, and where "dies" triggers fire.

Death triggers count this as dying.
vs

Exile

Removed from the game entirely. No graveyard, no death triggers, hard to get back.

The most permanent answer available.

Counter

Stops a spell on the stack before it resolves. Once a permanent is in play, it's too late to counter it.

Answers the cast, not the permanent.
vs

Removal

Deals with a permanent already in play. Destroy, exile, bounce, sacrifice, −X/−X.

Answers the permanent, not the cast.

Tap

Turns the card sideways. It can't attack, block, or use tap abilities until it untaps.

vs

Bounce

Returns a permanent to its owner's hand. Clears auras/counters and undoes the cast — but they can replay it.

Also called "return to hand."

Mill

Puts cards from the top of a library into the graveyard.

vs

Discard

Puts cards from a hand into the graveyard.

Same destination, different zone of origin.

Colorless

A card with no colored mana in its identity but which may still have a mana value.

vs

Devoid

A keyword making a card colorless regardless of its mana symbols — the symbols stay in the cost, but the card is colorless in every zone.

Matters for color-based protection and matters cards.

Triggers & Timing

the words that tell you when something happens
▾
ETB Slang
"Enters the battlefield." A trigger that fires when a permanent comes into play.
"When ~ enters, draw a card."
Dies Rules term
Specifically means "put into a graveyard from the battlefield." Exile doesn't trigger it.
LTB Slang
"Leaves the battlefield." Triggers on dying, exile, or bounce.
The Stack Zone
Where spells and abilities wait to resolve. Last in, first out — responses resolve before the thing they respond to.
Upkeep Phase
The step right after untap, before you draw. Many "at the beginning of your upkeep" triggers live here.
Cast Trigger Timing
Fires when a spell is cast — before it resolves, and even if it's later countered.
Attack Trigger Timing
Fires when a creature is declared as an attacker, whether or not it connects.
State-Based Actions Rules
Automatic checks the game constantly runs: 0-toughness creatures die, players at 0 life lose. No one has to "do" them.
Priority Rules
The right to act. Players pass priority in turn order; when all pass, the top of the stack resolves.
Replacement Effect Rules
Alters an event before it happens ("if it would… instead…"). Doesn't use the stack.

Casting & Cost Mechanics

alternative costs and ways spells reach the battlefield
▾
Devoid Keyword
The card is colorless, even though its mana cost may include colored symbols. Colorless in every zone.
Dodges "protection from black" and enables colorless-matters payoffs.
Flashback Keyword
Cast this from your graveyard for its flashback cost, then exile it.
Cascade Keyword
When cast, exile cards from your library until you hit a cheaper nonland, then cast it free.
Convoke Keyword
Tap creatures to help pay a spell's cost — each pays {1} or one mana of its color.
Delve Keyword
Exile cards from your graveyard, each paying for {1} of the cost.
Suspend Keyword
Exile with time counters for a cheaper cost; remove one each upkeep, then cast free with haste.
Overload Keyword
Pay the overload cost to make a targeted spell affect everything instead of one target.
Buyback Keyword
Pay extra so the spell returns to your hand instead of the graveyard.
Kicker Keyword
Pay an optional extra cost for a bonus effect when casting.
Escape Keyword
Cast from the graveyard by paying mana and exiling other cards from it.
Affinity Keyword
Costs {1} less for each of a named thing you control (usually artifacts).
X Spell Casting
A spell whose cost includes X, set as you cast it — scales with your mana.

Value & Mechanics

keywords that generate advantage or bend the rules
▾
Lifelink Evergreen
Whenever this deals damage, you gain that much life.
Fight Keyword action
Two creatures each deal damage equal to their power to the other. Not combat, so no evasion applies.
Sacrifice Outlet Slang
A permanent that lets you sacrifice creatures at will — key for dodging exile and enabling death triggers.
Blink / Flicker Slang
Exile a permanent and immediately return it, re-triggering ETB effects and dodging removal.
Reanimate Slang
Return a creature from a graveyard directly to the battlefield.
Scry N Keyword action
Look at the top N cards of your library; keep any on top or send them to the bottom.
Surveil N Keyword action
Like scry, but the choice is top or graveyard — feeds reanimation and graveyard synergies.
Explore Keyword action
Reveal the top card; if it's a land put it in hand, otherwise +1/+1 counter and choose to keep or bin it.
Ramp Slang
Accelerating your mana ahead of the curve via extra lands or mana rocks/dorks.
+1/+1 Counter Object
A permanent marker raising power and toughness. Sticks through most effects; removed only by specific answers.
Token Object
A permanent created by an effect with no card. Ceases to exist if it ever leaves the battlefield.
Cantrip Slang
A cheap spell that replaces itself by drawing a card — low cost, no card disadvantage.
Menace to Society Slang
Not a real rule — just a reminder that "goodstuff" value piles win games. (A wink in the margins.)
Extra Combat Effect
An additional combat phase in a turn — a common route to explosive commander damage.
Wheel Slang
An effect where each player discards their hand and draws a fresh new one (usually seven).
Tutor Slang
Any effect that searches your library for a specific card.

Common Keyword Counters

markers that grant abilities, seen constantly in modern Commander
▾
Stun Counter Counter
A permanent doesn't untap during its untap step; instead remove one stun counter.
Shield Counter Counter
If the permanent would be destroyed or dealt damage, remove a shield counter instead.
Finality Counter Counter
If a permanent with one would die, exile it instead.
Keyword Counter Counter
A counter (flying, deathtouch, etc.) that grants that ability while it's on the creature.

Commander-Specific

rules and terms unique to the format
▾
Commander Tax Format rule
Each time you recast your commander from the command zone, it costs an extra {2} per previous cast.
Commander Damage Format rule
21 combat damage from a single commander eliminates a player — tracked separately per commander.
Command Zone Zone
Where your commander starts and returns to. You may recast it from here instead of losing it to exile/graveyard.
Color Identity Deckbuilding
All mana symbols on your commander (cost + rules text) define which colors every card in your deck may use.
Partner Keyword
Lets you run two commanders, combining their color identities.
Background Keyword
A special enchantment a "choose a Background" commander can pair with, adding a second command-zone card.
Singleton Deckbuilding
Only one copy of any card (besides basic lands) allowed in a 100-card deck.
The 99 Slang
The 99 cards accompanying your commander.
Table Politics Slang
The social layer of multiplayer — deals, threat assessment, and not becoming the archenemy.
Archenemy Slang
The player the table collectively decides is the biggest threat — and ganks accordingly.
Pod / Power Level Slang
Your playgroup, and the rough strength bracket decks are matched at for a fair game.
